## Invoice: A Plain-English Definition


In everyday terms, an invoice is **a list of what you owe and a request to pay it.** It names the goods or services that were provided, shows the amount due, and asks the buyer to pay—usually by a stated date.


A few things are baked into the meaning:


- It’s **itemized** — it spells out what’s being charged for, not just a lump sum.
- It’s a **request for payment** — it comes _before_ the money is paid, not after.
- It follows **delivery** — it’s typically issued once the goods or services have been provided.


So when someone says “I sent you an invoice,” they mean: _here is a document listing what I provided and what you owe me for it._

## Invoice as a Noun vs a Verb


“Invoice” works as both a noun and a verb, and the difference trips people up.


- **Noun — _an invoice_.** The document itself: the itemized request for payment. _“I attached the invoice to my email.”_
- **Verb — _to invoice_.** The act of billing someone, i.e., sending them an invoice. _“I’ll invoice you next week.”_ or _“She invoiced the client for 10 hours of work.”_


To **invoice** someone is essentially to **bill** them. So “invoice me when you’re done” means “send me the invoice when you’re done,” and “we invoice clients monthly” means “we send clients an invoice each month.”

## Where the Word “Invoice” Comes From


The word traces back to French. According to [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/invoice), it comes from the French **_envoi_** (a “sending” or “dispatch”), from the verb **_envoyer_**, meaning **“to send.”** The English “invoice” entered use in the 1500s, originally as a plural-style form referring to a **list of goods that were sent or dispatched** to a buyer.


That origin still fits the modern meaning perfectly: an invoice began as an itemized list accompanying shipped goods, and over time it evolved into today’s document that itemizes what was provided and requests payment for it. The “sending” at the heart of _envoyer_ is why, even now, to _invoice_ someone means to _send_ them a request to pay.

## Invoice Meaning in Accounting


In accounting, “invoice” has a more precise sense. As [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/i/invoice.asp) describes it, an invoice is an **accounts-receivable record**—a source document that shows money is owed to the business and supports the amount recorded as revenue or as a receivable.


Definitionally, that gives an invoice two roles on the books:


- For the **seller**, an issued invoice is an **account receivable** (money expected to come in).
- For the **buyer**, a received invoice is an **account payable** (money expected to go out).


Because it documents a specific transaction, an invoice also functions as a **legal and audit record**—evidence of what was agreed, what was provided, and what was owed. (This is general information about how the term is used, not financial or legal advice.)

## Synonyms and Related Terms


Several words orbit “invoice.” Some are loose synonyms; others are specific types.


**Common synonyms (informal):** _bill_, _tab_. When a café hands you the “check” or you “run a tab,” that’s the casual cousin of an invoice. In business, the most common synonym is simply **bill**.


**Types of invoice (by direction):**

- **Sales invoice** — an invoice from the seller’s point of view (the seller _issues_ it to request payment).
- **Purchase invoice** — the very same document from the buyer’s point of view (the buyer _receives_ it as a bill to pay).

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What does “invoice” mean?


An invoice is an itemized commercial document a seller sends a buyer to request payment for goods or services already provided. As a verb, _to invoice_ means to bill someone—i.e., to send them that document.


### What is the definition of an invoice?


A document that lists goods or services provided, states the amount owed, and requests payment by a certain date. It’s a request for payment, issued before payment is made.


### What is the meaning of an invoice?


At its simplest, an invoice means “here’s what I provided, and here’s what you owe me for it.” It’s a formal, itemized way of asking to be paid.


### What is an invoice in simple terms?


It’s a bill: a list of what you bought or were provided, with the total you need to pay and when.


### Is an invoice a bill?


Essentially, yes. A bill and an invoice both ask for payment of money owed. “Invoice” is the more formal, business term; “bill” is the more casual, everyday word for the same idea.


### Invoice vs bill — what’s the difference?


They mean almost the same thing. The practical distinction is tone and context: “invoice” is the formal document a business issues, while “bill” is the everyday word (the restaurant bill, the phone bill). Same core meaning—a request to pay.


### What does “invoice” mean in accounting?


In accounting it’s a source document that records a transaction: an account receivable for the seller (money owed to them) and an account payable for the buyer (money they owe). It also serves as an audit and legal record of the deal.


### What does it mean when someone sends you an invoice?


It means they’re formally requesting payment for goods or services they provided. The invoice tells you what you’re being charged for, the total, and how and when to pay. (Receiving one doesn’t mean it’s been paid—an invoice is a request, not a confirmation.)


### Is “invoice” a noun or a verb?


Both. As a noun, an invoice is the payment-request document. As a verb, _to invoice_ means to bill someone—e.g., “I’ll invoice you next week.”


### Where does the word “invoice” come from?


From the French _envoi_ / _envoyer_, meaning “to send.” It originally referred to a list of goods that were sent to a buyer, and evolved into today’s payment-request document.


### What’s another word for “invoice”?


The closest everyday synonym is **bill**. Informally, people also say **tab**. In specific contexts you’ll see _sales invoice_ (seller’s side) and _purchase invoice_ (buyer’s side) for the same document.

## In Short


An invoice is an itemized request for payment a seller sends a buyer after providing goods or services. As a verb, to invoice is to bill. The word comes from the French for “to send,” and in accounting it’s an accounts-receivable record. It’s close to a _bill_, the opposite of a _receipt_, and different from a _statement_.
