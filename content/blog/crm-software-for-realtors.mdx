The best **CRM software for realtors** is not the one with the longest feature list—it is the cheapest tool that matches how you actually get leads and follow up with clients. A solo agent working referrals needs something very different from a five-person team buying leads off Zillow. So this guide skips the "one winner" myth. It explains what a real estate CRM does that a plain contact list can't, compares the top tools for 2026 by price and fit, and walks you through choosing one without paying for features you'll never touch.

CRM stands for customer relationship management—[the practices and software a business uses](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/customer_relation_management.asp) to track every lead and client interaction in one place. For an agent, that means every buyer inquiry, every past client, every "just checking in" you keep meaning to send, all in one system that reminds you before the lead goes cold. If you want the general fundamentals first, start with our [plain-English guide to CRM software](/blog/crm-software), then come back here for the real estate specifics.

## Why realtors need a CRM (not just a phone full of contacts)

Real estate runs on follow-up. Most deals don't close on the first call—they close after the fifth or fifteenth touch, months later. A phone full of names can't remind you that a buyer from last spring is finally pre-approved, or that a past client's kid is heading to college and they might downsize.

A CRM does three jobs a contact list can't:

- **Captures leads automatically** from your website, portal accounts, and open-house sign-ins, so nothing gets typed into a notebook and lost.
- **Automates follow-up** with drip campaigns and reminders, so warm leads stay warm and past clients hear from you on a schedule.
- **Tracks the pipeline** from new lead to under contract to closed, so you know where every deal stands at a glance.

This isn't a niche opinion. In the [2025 REALTORS® Technology Survey from the National Association of Realtors](https://www.nar.realtor/press-releases/realtors-embrace-ai-digital-tools-to-enhance-client-service-nar-survey-finds), CRM tools ranked second—behind only social media—for producing the highest number of quality leads, and brokerages were more likely to cover the cost of CRM software than any other tech tool. (Note that "Realtor" is a [trademarked term for members of the NAR](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/realtor); plenty of licensed agents who aren't members use the same tools.)

## What makes a real estate CRM different

You can run your business on a general CRM. Plenty of agents do. But a purpose-built real estate CRM comes with the plumbing already connected:

- **MLS and IDX integration** — pulls listing data and powers a search-enabled website so leads register to see homes.
- **Lead-source connections** — captures inquiries from Zillow, Realtor.com, Facebook, and your own site without manual entry.
- **Real estate drip campaigns** — pre-written buyer and seller nurture sequences you can turn on day one.
- **Transaction timelines** — checklists and dates from accepted offer through closing, so nothing slips in the 30-to-45-day scramble.

A general CRM (HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho) is a blank, flexible framework. It's cheaper and you can shape it however you like—but you build the real estate parts yourself. The trade-off is time versus money, and it's the core decision below.

**Follow Up Boss** is the pick most often named for teams. Its whole point is speed-to-lead and accountability: leads route to agents instantly, and managers see who followed up and who dropped the ball. It connects to 250-plus lead sources and tools. It is not the cheapest—it starts at about $69 per user per month with a 14-day trial per its <a href="https://www.followupboss.com/pricing" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">own pricing page</a>—and it's overkill for a solo agent doing referral business.

**Wise Agent** is the value champion for individuals. For a flat rate around $49 a month covering up to five users, you get contact management, transaction and commission tracking, and marketing automation in one place. It has been named a top real estate CRM by reviewers for several years running; see its <a href="https://wiseagent.com/pricing" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">current plans</a> for what's included.

**IXACT Contact** is one of the friendliest landings for a brand-new agent, largely because it has offered an unusually long free trial for the newly licensed—room to build habits before any bill arrives. It leans into keeping-in-touch marketing and a built-in agent website.

**Top Producer** is a long-standing name that has kept evolving. It's strong on lead nurturing and pulls MLS data directly, which some newer tools can't. It sits at the pricier end (around $179 per user per month), so it fits agents who will actually use the marketing depth.

**Lofty (formerly Chime)** and similar all-in-one platforms (kvCORE, CINC, Real Geeks) bundle lead generation, an IDX website, and AI-driven follow-up into one subscription—usually a custom quote in the hundreds per month. They fit teams that want their website, ads, and CRM from one vendor.

**General CRMs**—HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM—are the smart move if you'd rather pay less and configure the real estate bits yourself. HubSpot's free tier is a genuinely usable starting point; Pipedrive is the simplest visual pipeline; Zoho is the cheapest flexible option with the most add-ons. ## How to choose: two worked examples

**Example 1 — Solo agent, mostly referrals and past clients.** Maria closed 14 deals last year, almost all from referrals and her sphere. She doesn't buy online leads. She does not need a lead-routing team platform; she needs reliable keep-in-touch marketing and reminders. The right move: a value CRM like Wise Agent (or even HubSpot's free tier), set up with a monthly newsletter and a "call these 10 past clients" reminder each week. Spending $179 per user on Top Producer would buy lead-nurture firepower she has no leads to feed.

**Example 2 — Five-agent team buying portal leads.** Dev runs a team spending $4,000 a month on Zillow and Facebook leads. His bottleneck is speed-to-lead and accountability—leads sitting untouched for an hour are dead. Here the math flips: Follow Up Boss at roughly $69 per user (about $345/month for the team) instantly routes each lead, texts the agent, and shows Dev who's actually following up. The higher price is trivial next to the ad spend it protects. The cheap CRM that loses one lead a week costs far more than it saves.

The rule from both cases: **buy for your actual lead flow, not your ambitions.** Start at the cheapest tier that solves the problem you named out loud, and upgrade only when a specific limit is costing you deals.

## The money side a CRM won't handle

Here's the gap almost every "best real estate CRM" list skips: a CRM does not bill anyone. Your commission is paid through your brokerage at closing, so for that you don't invoice at all. But real estate careers are full of smaller payments a CRM ignores:

- **Referral fees** you send to or collect from another agent.
- **Transaction-coordinator or admin fees** if you do TC work on the side.
- **Property-management rent and leasing fees** if you manage units.
- **Consulting, staging, or BPO work** you bill hourly or per job.

## Common mistakes realtors make with a CRM

- **Buying the biggest platform first.** The most expensive lead-gen suite is wasted if you have no leads to nurture. Match the tool to today's business.
- **Never turning on automation.** A CRM you only open to look things up is a glorified contact list. The value is in drip campaigns and reminders that run without you.
- **Skipping data entry.** Garbage in, garbage out. If leads and notes don't get logged, no CRM can help. Pick one simple enough that you'll actually use it.
- **Paying per user for seats you don't fill.** Team platforms charge per user. Don't buy five seats for a two-agent team.
- **Confusing a lead source with a CRM.** Zillow sends leads; it doesn't nurture them. The CRM is where the follow-up lives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best CRM software for realtors?**
There is no single best CRM for every agent. Follow Up Boss is the most common pick for teams that live off online leads, Wise Agent is the value pick for solo agents, IXACT Contact is friendly for brand-new agents, and Top Producer is strong for lead nurturing with MLS data. The best one is the cheapest tool that fits how you actually get and follow up with clients.

**What CRM do most realtors use?**
There is no official count, but Follow Up Boss, Wise Agent, kvCORE/Lofty, and Top Producer are among the most widely used real estate CRMs. Many agents also use general CRMs like HubSpot, Pipedrive, or Zoho. What matters is not popularity but whether the tool matches your lead sources and follow-up habits.

**Is there a free CRM for real estate agents?**
HubSpot offers a genuinely usable free CRM you can adapt to real estate, and Zoho CRM has a free tier for very small teams. Purpose-built real estate CRMs are mostly paid, but many offer free trials—IXACT Contact, for example, has offered a six-month free trial for new agents. Try before you pay.

**How much does a real estate CRM cost?**
Pricing is usually per user, per month. Value tools start around $25 to $55 per user; team platforms like Follow Up Boss start around $69 per user; and lead-gen suites with IDX websites can run into the hundreds per month. Prices change often, so check the vendor's current pricing before you commit.

**What is the difference between a real estate CRM and a regular CRM?**
A real estate CRM adds features agents actually use: MLS and IDX website integration, drip campaigns built for buyers and sellers, transaction and closing timelines, and lead capture from portals like Zillow. A general CRM is a blank framework you configure yourself—cheaper and more flexible, but you build the real estate parts.

**Do I need a CRM as a new or solo agent?**
If you can track every lead and follow-up in your head, not yet. Once leads start slipping or you forget to follow up with past clients, a CRM pays for itself. Start with a free tool or a low-cost value CRM, and only upgrade when a specific limit is costing you deals.

Not really. CRMs are built to win and keep clients, not to bill.
