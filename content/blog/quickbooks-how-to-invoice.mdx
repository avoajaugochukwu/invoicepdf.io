The fastest way to invoice a customer in QuickBooks Online: click **+ New** in the top-left corner, choose **Invoice**, pick the customer, add your products or services, then hit **Save and send**. That is the whole loop. Everything below fills in the details—each field, how sending works, how to record the payment when it lands, and the same flow for QuickBooks Desktop.

An [invoice](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/i/invoice.asp) is just an itemized request for payment: who owes what, for which work, and by when. QuickBooks builds one for you and then tracks whether it gets paid. Let's walk it through.

---

## Before you start

You need two things in place, or the invoice screen will feel empty:

- **A customer record.** QuickBooks pulls the name, email, and billing address from the customer's profile. You can add a new customer on the fly, but it's cleaner to set them up first under **Sales → Customers**.
- **Products or services set up as items.** Each line on an invoice points to an item (for example "Consulting — hourly" or "Logo design"). Set these under **Sales → Products & services** so the description and rate fill in automatically. You can also type a one-off line by hand.

Have those ready and the invoice takes about a minute.

## How to create an invoice in QuickBooks Online

This is the modern, browser-based version most new users have. The official steps from [Intuit's create-invoices help page](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/invoicing/create-invoices-quickbooks-online/L7gSzvCld_US_en_US) match what's below:

1. **Open a new invoice.** Click **+ New** in the top-left corner, then **Invoice**. (You can also go **Sales → Invoices → New invoice**.)
2. **Choose the customer.** Use the **Add customer** dropdown. QuickBooks fills in the saved email and address. Pick **Add new** if they're not in your list yet, and double-check the email—that's where the invoice goes.
3. **Check the dates and terms.** Confirm the **Invoice date**, then set **Terms** (for example Net 30). QuickBooks calculates the **Due date** from the terms. Net 30 means payment is due 30 days after the invoice date—not the 30th of the month.
4. **Add your line items.** Under **Product or service**, pick an item from the dropdown, then set the **Description**, **Qty**, **Rate**, and **Amount**. Add a row for each thing you're billing. QuickBooks totals it for you.
5. **Add tax and discounts if they apply.** If you've turned on sales tax, choose the rate. Whether you charge sales tax depends on your state and what you're selling—many services aren't taxed—so don't assume it applies.
6. **Turn on payment options (optional).** Click **Payment options** and switch on the methods you want—card, bank transfer, and so on. If prompted, you sign up for QuickBooks Payments so customers can pay online straight from the invoice.
7. **Save or send.** Click **Save and send** to email it now, **Save and close** to send later, or use the review screen to send a pay-by-text link.

That's a complete invoice. QuickBooks assigns the invoice number automatically and increments it each time—leave that alone. Never reuse or manually duplicate an invoice number, even for one you voided, or your records and any audit trail get tangled.

## How to send the invoice

If you clicked **Save and send**, QuickBooks opens an email preview. Confirm the address, edit the subject or message if you like, and send. The customer receives either a PDF or a link to an online invoice they can pay from directly.

After it goes out, QuickBooks tracks the status for you on the **Invoices** list—**Sent**, then **Viewed** when the customer opens it, **Partial** if they pay part, and **Paid** once it's settled. You can also switch on **automatic reminders** so QuickBooks nudges late payers without you writing the email.

## How to create an invoice in QuickBooks Desktop

QuickBooks Desktop is the installed program (Pro, Premier, or Enterprise). The fields are identical; only the navigation changes. Intuit's [Desktop invoice guide](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/invoicing/create-invoice-quickbooks-desktop/L0J64E3xu_US_en_US) lays it out:

1. From the **Home** screen or the top menu, go **Customers → Create Invoices**.
2. In the **Customer: Job** dropdown, choose the customer. Add a new one if needed.
3. Confirm the **Date**, **Invoice #**, and **Terms**.
4. Fill in the line items—**Item**, **Description**, **Qty**, **Rate**, **Amount**.
5. Click **Save & Close**, or **Email** to send it right away.

To let Desktop customers pay online, set it up once under **Edit → Preferences → Payments → Company Preferences**. To record payments later, you'll use **Customers → Receive Payments**.

## How to record a payment (mark an invoice as paid)

Creating the invoice is only half the job—your books stay wrong until you record the money coming in. In QuickBooks Online, open the invoice and click **Receive payment**, then enter the **amount**, **date**, and **payment method**. QuickBooks matches it to the invoice and reduces your accounts receivable balance automatically. Intuit's [record-invoice-payments guide](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/invoicing/record-invoice-payments-quickbooks-online/L4ZadDW7F_US_en_US) covers partial payments and deposits too. In Desktop, do the same through **Customers → Receive Payments**.

Do **not** just delete the invoice or type "PAID" on it. Recording the payment is what keeps income, accounts receivable, and your reports accurate.

## Customizing your invoice template

Set the look once and every future invoice inherits it. In QuickBooks Online, go to **Settings** (the gear icon) **→ Custom form styles**, then edit the default or make a new style. You can:

- **Upload your logo** and pick an accent color.
- **Choose which columns show** (for example hide the Qty column on flat-fee work, or show SKU).
- **Edit the footer and payment terms wording** so it reads the way you want.
- **Add a custom message** that appears on every invoice—handy for "Thanks for your business" or your payment instructions.

QuickBooks Desktop handles this under **Lists → Templates**, where you can duplicate and edit an invoice layout. A tidy, consistent template looks more professional and tends to get paid faster than a bare one.

## Worked example 1: a one-off freelance invoice

Say you're a freelance designer billing a new client, Bright Coffee Co., for a logo.

1. **+ New → Invoice**, then **Add customer → Bright Coffee Co.** (added as a new customer, email `owner@brightcoffee.com`).
2. **Terms: Net 15.** Invoice date August 22, so the due date fills in as September 6.
3. **Line 1:** item "Logo design," Qty 1, Rate $600, Amount $600.
4. **Line 2:** item "Revisions — hourly," Qty 2, Rate $75, Amount $150.
5. Subtotal $750. No sales tax (a design service, not taxable in this client's state).
6. **Payment options:** card and bank transfer switched on.
7. **Save and send.**

The client gets an online invoice, pays by card on August 28, and you click **Receive payment** for $750. Status flips to **Paid**, and your income and cash accounts update on their own.

## Worked example 2: a partial payment on a bigger job

Now a contractor bills Maple Street LLC $4,000 for a kitchen remodel, Net 30.

1. Create the invoice for $4,000 with two line items (labor $2,500, materials $1,500).
2. The client pays a $2,000 deposit on day 10. Open the invoice, **Receive payment → $2,000**. Status shows **Partial**, with a $2,000 balance remaining.
3. On day 25 the client pays the rest. **Receive payment → $2,000.** Status flips to **Paid**.

Because you recorded each payment against the same invoice, QuickBooks always shows exactly how much Maple Street still owes—no guessing, no second invoice.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sending before checking the email address.** QuickBooks sends to whatever is on the customer record. A typo means the invoice never arrives and you wonder why you're not paid.
- **Confusing Net terms with a calendar date.** Net 30 is 30 days from the invoice date. Set the terms and let QuickBooks compute the due date—don't hand-type "due the 30th."
- **Marking paid without recording the payment.** Deleting the invoice or overwriting it breaks your accounts receivable. Always use **Receive payment**.
- **Editing or reusing invoice numbers.** Let QuickBooks auto-number. Reusing a number—even a voided one—creates duplicates that confuse you, your client, and any auditor.
- **Assuming you must add sales tax.** Tax depends on your state and what you sell. This is general information, not tax advice—check your state's rules or your accountant before charging it.
- **Not keeping copies.** Whether you use QuickBooks or not, keep your invoices. The [IRS advises businesses to retain supporting records](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping) like sales invoices for their filings.

## The short version

Invoicing in QuickBooks is three moves: **create** (+ New → Invoice, pick the customer, add line items), **send** (Save and send, then let QuickBooks track the status), and **record the payment** (Receive payment when the money lands). Desktop uses the same fields under **Customers → Create Invoices**. Get those three steps down and your accounts receivable stays honest without extra work.
