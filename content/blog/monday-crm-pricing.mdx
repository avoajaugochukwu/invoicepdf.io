![Monday CRM pricing tiers compared for a small business, showing per-seat costs and the 3-seat minimum](/blog/monday-crm-pricing/featured.webp)

**Monday CRM costs $12 to $28 per seat per month when billed annually**, across three published tiers—Basic ($12), Standard ($17), and Pro ($28)—plus a custom-quoted Ultimate tier. Pay month-to-month and those same plans cost roughly $18, $25, and $41 per seat. There is a catch that the sticker price never shows: every paid plan has a **3-seat minimum**, so your smallest possible bill is three seats, not one. Prices below are from <a href="https://monday.com/crm/pricing" rel="nofollow">monday.com's own CRM pricing page</a> and are current as of this writing; vendors change pricing often, so confirm before you buy.

This guide breaks down what each tier really costs once the minimums and billing traps are counted, walks two real-team examples, and helps you pick the tier you actually need instead of the one the "Most Popular" badge nudges you toward.

## Monday CRM pricing at a glance

Monday sells its CRM as a separate product from its Work Management, Service, and other tools, so this pricing applies to the CRM specifically. All per-seat prices assume the 3-seat minimum.

| Plan | Annual (per seat/mo) | Monthly (per seat/mo) | Active contacts | Quotes & invoices/mo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | $12 | ~$18 | 1,000 | 20 |
| Standard | $17 | ~$25 | 10,000 | 50 |
| Pro | $28 | ~$41 | 100,000 | 250 |
| Ultimate | Custom | Custom | Unlimited | Unlimited |

**The headline numbers per year, at the 3-seat minimum:** Basic is about $432/year, Standard about $612/year, and Pro about $1,008/year—billed annually. Choose monthly billing and those become roughly $648, $900, and $1,476. Same software, hundreds of dollars apart.

## The costs the sticker price hides

A [customer relationship management](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/customer_relationship_management.asp) tool is sold per seat, and the per-seat number is only the start. Three things quietly inflate the bill.

**The 3-seat minimum.** Even a solo operator pays for three seats. On Basic that is 3 × $12 × 12 = **$432 a year minimum**, whether one person logs in or three do. Monday runs a 14-day free trial but has no free plan for the CRM product—so once the trial ends, the floor is three paid seats.

**The annual-vs-monthly gap.** Monday advertises a discount for paying yearly, and it is not small. On Pro the difference is $28 vs about $41 per seat per month—roughly $156 per seat per year. For a five-person team that is nearly $800 a year for the "convenience" of paying monthly. If you are confident you will keep the tool, annual almost always wins; if you are still testing, eat the monthly premium until you are sure.

**Paid seats for people who only look.** Some CRMs give you free view-only accounts. Monday CRM does not. An executive who wants a dashboard glance, or a client who wants project status, still needs a full paid seat. This is the single most common reason a team's real spend lands 30–40% above the quote they first ran—so count the lurkers, not just the doers.

**Seats sold in blocks.** Monday prices seats in fixed buckets rather than one at a time. Adding a fourth or fifth person can push you into a higher block than you expected, so the marginal cost of "just one more user" is sometimes larger than the per-seat rate suggests.

## Worked example 1: a 3-person sales team

Say you run a small agency with three people who all touch deals. You want automation and a few dashboards, so **Standard** fits.

- Annual billing: 3 seats × $17 × 12 = **$612/year**
- Monthly billing: 3 seats × $25 × 12 = **$900/year**

Paying monthly costs you **$288 more a year** for identical software. That gap alone is bigger than a full extra seat on the annual plan. Unless cash flow forces monthly, annual is the obvious call here.

## Worked example 2: a 5-person team with a watching boss

Now say five reps need Pro for mass email and forecasting, and the owner wants a read-only dashboard. Because Monday has no free viewer seat, the owner counts as a sixth paid seat.

- 6 seats × $28 × 12 = **$2,016/year** on annual billing
- The same six seats billed monthly: 6 × $41 × 12 = **$2,952/year**

The read-only owner adds $336/year on annual billing for a dashboard they glance at once a week. That is a legitimate reason to ask whether that person truly needs a live seat or could get a weekly exported report instead.

## Which tier do you actually need?

Ignore the "Most Popular" badge and match the tier to a real limit you are hitting.

- **Basic** — a tiny team that just wants shared contacts and a pipeline, under 1,000 contacts, and little automation. Fine as a starting point; you will feel the 20-quotes/month and 5-columns-per-board caps quickly if you grow.
- **Standard** — the honest starting point for most small teams. Automations (a few hundred a month), more dashboards, 10,000 contacts, and 50 quotes/invoices cover normal operations.
- **Pro** — you need mass email, email sequences, sales forecasting, or you are past Standard's automation and contact limits. This is where Monday CRM becomes a real sales engine rather than a shared spreadsheet.
- **Ultimate** — enterprise security, advanced permissions, HIPAA compliance, and unlimited limits. Custom-quoted, so treat any number a rep gives you as negotiable.

A quick rule: pick the **lowest** tier that clears the one limit you are actually hitting today, not the tier that clears limits you imagine hitting next year. You can upgrade in a click; you cannot un-spend the money.

## Common mistakes when pricing Monday CRM

**Budgeting for one seat.** The 3-seat minimum makes "$12/month" really "$432/year." Always multiply by three (or your real headcount) before you compare it to anything.

**Upgrading a whole plan for one feature.** Teams jump from Standard to Pro just to lift the quotes-and-invoices cap or get a bit more automation—paying $11 more per seat per month across every user for one limit. Before you upgrade the entire team, ask whether the thing you need is worth $11 × every seat × 12.

**Paying a CRM to send invoices.** Monday CRM caps quotes and invoices per tier (20, 50, 250) and its invoicing is basic. If billing is the pinch, you do not need to climb a CRM tier for it. Our free [invoice generator](/invoice-generator) makes clean, professional invoices and PDFs with no seat count and no monthly cap, and our [invoice templates](/templates) cover the common formats. Keep Monday for the pipeline; bill with a tool built for billing. (If you are unsure whether a document should be a quote or an invoice, see our guide on [quotes vs invoices](/blog/quote-vs-invoice).)

**Forgetting the subscription is deductible.** A CRM <a href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/subscription">subscription</a> used for your business is an ordinary business expense. In the US, the IRS lets you deduct ordinary and necessary [business expenses](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/deducting-business-expenses), and software subscriptions typically qualify—reported for a sole proprietor on [Schedule C](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-c-form-1040). This is general information, not tax advice; confirm your own situation with a professional.

## Do you even need a CRM yet?

If you are losing leads and follow-ups across email threads and sticky notes, a CRM earns its price fast, and Monday is a solid, easy-to-learn option—compare it against the field in our roundup of the [best CRM management software](/blog/best-crm-management-software) and our primer on [what CRM software does](/blog/crm-software). But if your customer list is short and orderly and your only real problem is getting invoices out and getting paid, a CRM is an expensive answer to the wrong question. Start the 14-day trial with your own data, watch whether your team actually updates it, and only then commit three seats' worth of budget.

The short version: Monday CRM is $12–$28 per seat per month annually, three seats minimum, no free plan, and paid seats even for people who only watch. Price it at your real headcount, pay annually if you are staying, and do not buy a sales engine when you needed an invoice.
