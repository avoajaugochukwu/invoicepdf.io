## What an invoice maker actually does

An [invoice](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/i/invoice.asp) is the document you send after work is done to request payment. An invoice maker is just a form that turns your inputs into that document and saves it as a file. You type in:

- **Your details** — your name (or business name), address, and email.
- **Your client's details** — who is being billed.
- **A unique invoice number** — for your records and theirs.
- **Dates** — the invoice date and the due date.
- **Line items** — each service or product, with a quantity and a rate.

The tool multiplies quantity by rate, adds up the lines, applies any tax or discount, and shows a formatted invoice. When it looks right, you download the PDF or email it. That's the whole job. A good one doesn't ask you to install anything or create an account.

The output is the important part. A plain generator produces the file and stops there—tracking who has paid is up to you. That's the line between a *maker* (produce the file, done) and full *invoicing software* (an account that stores and tracks everything). For most freelancers and small businesses, the maker plus a simple spreadsheet is all you need.

## Why PDF is the format to send

You could send an invoice as a Word document or an Excel sheet, but you shouldn't. Here's why PDF wins.

A [PDF](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/about-adobe-pdf.html) (Portable Document Format) locks the layout, fonts, and numbers in place. Open it on a phone, a Mac, a Windows PC, or print it—it looks identical every time. A Word or Excel file can reflow on the client's computer if they don't have the same fonts, and, worse, the totals sit in editable cells that anyone can change. That's a bad look on a payment request.

PDF also reads as professional. It's the default business document format, so a PDF invoice signals that you know what you're doing. And it's easy for the client to file, forward to their accountant, or attach to their own records without anything breaking.

So the rule is simple: build the invoice however you like, but **send it as a PDF**.

## How to make a PDF invoice: a worked example

Say you're **Maria Lopez**, a freelance web designer, billing a client for a landing page. Here's the whole flow in an invoice maker.

**1. Add your details (the "from").** Maria isn't incorporated, so she invoices under her legal name—[you don't need a registered business to invoice](/blog/how-to-write-an-invoice-for-beginners).

```text
Maria Lopez
482 Oak Street, Austin, TX 78701
maria@example.com
```

**2. Add the client (the "bill to").**

```text
Brightway Coffee Co.
attn: Sam Rivera
1120 Congress Ave, Austin, TX 78701
```

**3. Number it and set dates.** Give it a unique number—[never reuse an invoice number](/blog/what-is-an-invoice-number), even for a canceled or corrected invoice. She uses `INV-2026-014`. Invoice date: September 9, 2026. Terms: **Net 15**, which means payment is due 15 days *after the invoice date*—so the due date is September 24, 2026.

**4. Add line items.** The maker does the multiplication and the total.

| Description | Qty | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page design | 1 | $1,200 | $1,200 |
| Mobile responsive build | 1 | $600 | $600 |
| Stock photo licensing | 3 | $15 | $45 |
| **Total** | | | **$1,845** |

**5. Handle tax honestly.** Maria's work is a service. Whether she adds sales tax depends on her state and the type of work—there's no federal sales tax in the US, and many services aren't taxed at all. This is general information, not tax advice; the [IRS Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) is a good starting point, and your state's rules settle the sales-tax question. In Maria's case she leaves it off.

**6. Add payment instructions.** "Pay by bank transfer to…" or "Zelle to maria@example.com." Clear instructions get you paid faster.

**7. Preview and download the PDF.** Check the numbers, click download, and email the file. Done—no account, no charge.

## Already have an invoice? Turn it into a PDF

Maybe you already built an invoice in Word, Excel, or Google Docs and just need it as a PDF. You have three easy options:

- **Save As / Export.** In Microsoft Word or Excel, choose **File → Save As** (or **Export**) and pick **PDF** as the file type. Done in two clicks.
- **Google Docs or Sheets.** Choose **File → Download → PDF Document (.pdf)**.
- **Print to PDF.** Open the file, choose **Print**, and select **Save as PDF** as the printer. Every modern browser and operating system supports this, so it works even for a file that has no export button.

## What makes a PDF invoice look professional

The format is only half the job. The content is the other half. A clean PDF invoice includes:

- **A clear header** — the word "Invoice," your name or logo, and your contact details.
- **The client's details** — billed to whom, at what address.
- **A unique invoice number** — so both sides can reference it later.
- **Dates** — issued on, and due by.
- **Itemized lines** — each charge described plainly, with quantity, rate, and amount.
- **A total that stands out** — the number the client actually needs to pay.
- **Payment instructions** — how and where to pay.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sending an editable file.** A Word or Excel attachment can be changed and can break on the client's screen. Always send the PDF.
- **Reusing an invoice number.** Every invoice needs a unique one, even after a void or a correction—duplicates break your records at tax time and confuse the client.
- **Misreading the terms.** "Net 15" means 15 days *from the invoice date*, not by the 15th of the month.
- **Leaving off the due date.** "Due upon receipt" or a firm date both beat silence. No date, no urgency.
- **Assuming you must charge sales tax.** It's state- and transaction-dependent; don't add it by reflex, and don't assume it's never owed either.
- **Downloading a watermarked PDF.** Some "free" tools stamp their logo on the file unless you pay. Check the preview—a professional invoice shouldn't advertise the tool that made it.
- **Putting your Social Security number on the invoice.** It isn't a required field and it's an identity-theft risk. If a client needs your taxpayer ID for their [recordkeeping](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping), that belongs on a W-9, not on the invoice.
