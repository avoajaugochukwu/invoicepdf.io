## What every billing invoice needs first

Before the examples, here is the short checklist they all share. Miss one of these and payment slows down.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Your name / business + contact | So the client knows who to pay and how to reach you |
| "Bill To" — client name and address | Ties the invoice to the right payer for their records |
| Invoice number | A unique reference for tracking; never reuse one, even for a voided invoice |
| Invoice date | The day you issued it—this is what payment terms count from |
| Due date | A specific calendar date, not just "Net 30" |
| Itemized lines | One row per thing you are charging for, with amounts |
| Total due | The bold number the client looks for first |
| Payment instructions | How to pay: bank transfer, PayPal, check, card link |

A note on sales tax: whether you add a tax line depends on your state and what you are selling. Many services are not taxable, and the US does not require one federal invoice format—the fields above are practical, not a legal mandate. Verify your own situation with your state's revenue department. (General information, not tax advice.)

Keep a copy of every invoice you send. The [IRS expects small businesses to keep records that support their income](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep), and sent invoices are exactly those records.

## Example 1: Freelance project (flat fee)

The most common billing invoice. You quoted a price, did the work, and now you bill for it in a few clear lines.

**Scenario:** A freelance designer bills a client for a completed logo package.

| Description | Qty | Rate | Line total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logo design — 3 initial concepts | 1 | $450.00 | $450.00 |
| Revision rounds | 2 | $75.00 | $150.00 |
| Final files + brand color guide (PDF) | 1 | $100.00 | $100.00 |
| **Subtotal** | | | **$700.00** |
| Sales tax | | | — |
| **Total due** | | | **$700.00** |

```text
Invoice #INV-018
Issued: Aug 16, 2026   Due: Aug 30, 2026 (Net 14)
Bill To: Harper Coffee Co., 210 Mill St, Austin, TX 78701
Pay via: bank transfer (details below) or PayPal to maya@example.com
Thank you for your business!
```

**Why it works:** each line is one deliverable, so the client can see exactly what they paid for. The due date is a real date, not a vague "end of month."

## Example 2: Hourly consulting

When you bill for time, the rate and hours have to be visible. This is the format lawyers, accountants, consultants, and developers use.

**Scenario:** A marketing consultant bills for a month of advisory hours.

| Date | Description | Hours | Rate | Line total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 4 | Strategy call + campaign audit | 3.0 | $120.00 | $360.00 |
| Aug 11 | Landing-page copy review | 2.5 | $120.00 | $300.00 |
| Aug 19 | Analytics setup + reporting | 4.0 | $120.00 | $480.00 |
| **Subtotal** | | **9.5** | | **$1,140.00** |
| **Total due** | | | | **$1,140.00** |

```text
Invoice #2026-041
Issued: Aug 16, 2026   Due: Sep 15, 2026 (Net 30)
Bill To: Northwind SaaS, 88 Harbor Ave, Seattle, WA 98101
Terms: Net 30. Late payments accrue 1.5%/month.
Pay via: ACH transfer or card link below.
```

**Why it works:** dated rows let the client match each entry to work they remember. The hours total at the bottom is easy to sanity-check against the rate. Not sure what "Net 30" means? See [what Net 30 means on an invoice](/blog/what-does-net-30-mean-on-an-invoice).

## Example 3: Contractor / trade job (labor + materials)

Trade invoices split labor from materials so the client sees where the money went. This is standard for handymen, cleaners, mechanics, and general contractors.

**Scenario:** A handyman bills for a half-day repair job.

| Description | Qty | Rate | Line total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labor — repairs & fixture install | 4 hrs | $65.00 | $260.00 |
| Materials — faucet + supply lines | 1 | $92.00 | $92.00 |
| Materials — drywall patch kit | 1 | $18.00 | $18.00 |
| **Subtotal** | | | **$370.00** |
| Sales tax (materials, TX 8.25%) | | | $9.08 |
| **Total due** | | | **$379.08** |

```text
Invoice #H-2291
Issued: Aug 16, 2026   Due on receipt
Bill To: J. Ramirez, 45 Oak Lane, Dallas, TX 75201
Pay via: check, Venmo, or card on site.
```

**Why it works:** materials are itemized separately, so a taxable-goods line can carry the tax while the labor line stays clean. ## Example 4: Product / retail sale

When you sell physical goods, quantity and unit price do the heavy lifting, and a tax line is more often in play.

**Scenario:** A small candle maker bills a boutique for a wholesale order.

| SKU | Description | Qty | Unit price | Line total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CN-100 | Soy candle, 8oz — Cedar | 24 | $9.00 | $216.00 |
| CN-104 | Soy candle, 8oz — Citrus | 18 | $9.00 | $162.00 |
| CN-220 | Gift set (3-pack) | 6 | $22.00 | $132.00 |
| **Subtotal** | | | | **$510.00** |
| Wholesale discount (10%) | | | | −$51.00 |
| **Total due** | | | | **$459.00** |

```text
Invoice #WS-0087
Issued: Aug 16, 2026   Due: Aug 26, 2026 (Net 10)
Bill To: The Fern Shop, 12 Market St, Portland, OR 97204
Ship via: UPS Ground. Payment due before ship date.
```

**Why it works:** SKUs let both sides reconcile the order against inventory, and the discount is shown as its own line so the boutique sees the deal it got. For deeper line-item guidance, see the [itemized invoice guide](/blog/itemized-invoice).

## Example 5: Recurring / retainer (monthly)

Recurring billing invoices look like any other invoice, but the description names the period so it never gets confused with last month's.

**Scenario:** A bookkeeper bills a client for a monthly retainer.

| Description | Qty | Rate | Line total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly bookkeeping retainer — August 2026 | 1 | $600.00 | $600.00 |
| Extra: year-end catch-up entries | 1 | $150.00 | $150.00 |
| **Total due** | | | **$750.00** |

```text
Invoice #R-2026-08
Issued: Aug 16, 2026   Due: Aug 23, 2026 (Net 7)
Bill To: Cedar & Co., 500 Vine St, Cincinnati, OH 45202
Recurring: 1st of each month. Auto-charge card on file.
```

**Why it works:** naming the month in the line item makes each invoice self-explanatory in the client's files, and the "recurring" note sets the expectation for next month.

## Example 6: Past-due reminder

When an invoice goes unpaid, you resend it as a reminder—same invoice number, new note, and any agreed late fee.

**Scenario:** The freelance designer from Example 1 follows up 10 days after the due date.

```text
Invoice #INV-018 — PAST DUE
Original due: Aug 30, 2026   Now: Sep 9, 2026
Balance: $700.00
Late fee (1.5%): $10.50
Amount now due: $710.50

Hi Harper — a quick reminder that invoice #INV-018 is now past due.
Please send payment at your earliest convenience. Let me know if
there's any issue on your end. Thank you!
```

**Why it works:** it keeps the original invoice number (so records stay linked), states the new total plainly, and stays polite. For wording that gets results, see [how to ask for payment](/blog/how-to-ask-for-payment).

One more type worth knowing: a **commercial invoice** for shipping goods across borders. It carries extra fields for customs, and the format is defined by trade authorities, not by you—see the [commercial invoice guidance from trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/commercial-invoice) and our own [commercial invoice guide](/blog/what-is-a-commercial-invoice).

## Common mistakes these examples avoid

- **No due date.** "Net 30" alone is not a date. Write the specific day so there is no ambiguity.
- **Reusing an invoice number.** Every invoice gets a fresh, unique number—even one you voided. Duplicates wreck your records and confuse clients.
- **Vague line items.** "Consulting — $1,140" invites questions. Dated, itemized rows get paid faster.
- **Assuming you must charge sales tax.** Tax is state- and product-dependent; many services are not taxed. Check before adding a tax line.
- **Thinking a billing invoice is a receipt.** An invoice asks for payment; a receipt confirms it. See [invoice vs. receipt](/blog/invoice-vs-receipt) and [invoice vs. bill](/blog/invoice-vs-bill) if the terms blur together.
- **Waiting to send.** Invoice the day the work is done. Every day you delay is a day later you get paid.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a billing invoice?**

A billing invoice is a document a business sends to a customer to request payment for goods or services. "Billing invoice" and "invoice" mean the same thing—the word "billing" just emphasizes that it is a request for money owed. It lists what was delivered, the amount due, and how and when to pay.

**What should a billing invoice include?**

Your name and contact details, the client's name, a unique invoice number, the issue date and due date, an itemized list of what you are charging for with amounts, the total due, and payment instructions. Add a sales-tax line only if the sale is taxable in your state.

**What is the difference between a billing invoice and a receipt?**

A billing invoice requests payment before money changes hands. A receipt confirms payment after it has been received. They can look almost identical, but one asks for money and the other proves it was paid. See [invoice vs. receipt](/blog/invoice-vs-receipt) for the full comparison.

**Is a billing invoice the same as a bill?**

Roughly, yes. A bill and an invoice are the same document seen from two sides: the seller calls it an invoice, and the customer who has to pay it often calls it a bill. The content is identical. See [invoice vs. bill](/blog/invoice-vs-bill) for the nuance.
