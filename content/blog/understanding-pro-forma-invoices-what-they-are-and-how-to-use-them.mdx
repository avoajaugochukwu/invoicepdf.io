### What is a Pro Forma Invoice?


A **pro forma invoice** is a preliminary document provided by a seller to a buyer before goods or services are delivered. Think of it as an estimate that outlines the details of the transaction, such as price, quantity, and description of the goods. It's not a demand for payment; it’s simply a way to communicate the expected costs before the final sale goes through.


**Real-life scenario**: Suppose you’re importing goods from overseas. The supplier sends you a pro forma invoice to confirm the order’s details – like a "preview" before the final invoice is issued.


**What a pro forma invoice covers**:

- **Itemized list of goods or services**
- **Expected costs** (including shipping, taxes, and handling charges)
- **Terms of sale** (e.g., payment terms, shipping method)

**What it’s not**:

- It's **not a bill** or demand for payment
- It doesn’t represent a **completed transaction**
- It’s not used for **accounting** until the final invoice is issued

---


### Pro Forma Invoice vs. Commercial Invoice


The key difference between a pro forma invoice and a commercial invoice lies in their timing and purpose.


A **commercial invoice** is the final, legally binding document issued after goods or services are delivered. It's used for official purposes, such as customs clearance, and contains detailed transaction information (buyer and seller info, payment terms, and total amount).


On the other hand, a **pro forma invoice** is a preliminary document, used as an estimate of what the final costs will be. It’s like a “preview” of the official invoice that will follow once the transaction is completed.


**Real-life scenario**: If you're purchasing equipment from another country, the pro forma invoice will give you an idea of the total costs, including shipping. The commercial invoice, however, will be issued once the goods are shipped and used for payment processing and customs purposes.

### How to Create a Pro Forma Invoice


Creating a pro forma invoice is easier than you might think. Here’s a step-by-step guide:

1. **Start with the basics**: Include the names and contact details of both the buyer and seller.
2. **List the items**: Clearly describe the goods or services, their quantity, and unit price.
3. **Include additional costs**: Don’t forget to factor in shipping, handling, or tax charges.
4. **Specify the payment terms**: State when payment is expected, whether upon receipt of goods or on a specific date.
5. **Double-check**: Make sure all the details are accurate to avoid any confusion later.

**Real-life scenario**: If you’re purchasing office furniture from overseas, your pro forma invoice will outline the cost of each piece, shipping fees, and taxes. It will also specify the payment terms, typically after you’ve received the goods.

### When Should You Use a Pro Forma Invoice?


Pro forma invoices are commonly used when you need a clear understanding of expected costs before finalizing a purchase. This is particularly important in international transactions, where shipping costs, taxes, or other fees can vary.


**Real-life scenario**: If you're importing goods for your business, you’ll likely receive a pro forma invoice to confirm the price, terms, and shipping details before proceeding with the order. It ensures both the buyer and seller are on the same page before moving forward.


**Pro forma invoices are used for**:

- **Pre-sale communication** between buyers and sellers
- **Confirming transaction details** in international trade
- **Providing an estimate** for customs or import duties

**When you don’t need a pro forma invoice**:

- When the goods or services are **already delivered**, and the final invoice is issued
- For simple **domestic transactions** that don’t require detailed estimates

### Common Mistakes When Using Pro Forma Invoices

1. **Thinking a pro forma invoice is the same as a commercial invoice**

    _A pro forma invoice is an estimate, not a bill. It’s used before the transaction is complete and doesn’t require immediate payment._

2. **Using a pro forma invoice for customs clearance**

    _Customs only accepts commercial invoices. Be sure to use the correct documentation for international shipments to avoid delays at the border._

3. **Assuming pro forma invoices are always necessary**

    _Not every international transaction requires a pro forma invoice. It’s mainly used for estimates or when detailed transaction info is needed beforehand._


**Do this instead**:

- Only use pro forma invoices when you need an **estimate** or a preview of costs.
- Use **commercial invoices** for actual shipments and customs clearance.

### Frequently Asked Questions


**What’s the difference between a pro forma invoice and a commercial invoice?**


A pro forma invoice is an estimate provided before goods or services are delivered, while a commercial invoice is the official bill issued once the transaction is completed.


**Do I need to pay for items listed on a pro forma invoice?**


No, a pro forma invoice is not a demand for payment. It’s simply an estimate of what the final cost will be once the sale is completed.


**Can a pro forma invoice be used for customs?**


No, customs requires a commercial invoice, as it’s the official document outlining the transaction and value of goods being shipped.
